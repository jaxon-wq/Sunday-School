Teachers —

Starting this week, I want to send you a few resources each Sunday as you prepare to teach. Please know I’m not telling you what to teach — not in any way. That belongs to you and the Lord, for the class in front of you.

This lesson can go so many beautiful directions. I’ve sat with the materials and gathered three themes I personally would lean into — one of them, or all three. Take what helps. Leave what doesn’t. I’m simply here to put a few good tools in your hands as you do this remarkable work.

 Theme

The Lord inspires people to accomplish His purposes

“the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia…”

Open Ezra 1:1 in Gospel Library →

Primary source · outside the Bible

The Cyrus Cylinder

c. 539 BC · British Museum · baked clay, inscribed in Akkadian

Cyrus speaking in the first person — translated from the cuneiform:

“I returned to (these) sacred cities… the sanctuaries of which have been ruins for a long time… I (also) gathered all their former inhabitants and returned (to them) their habitations… upon the command of Marduk, the great lord.”

Primary source · Josephus

Antiquities of the Jews, Book 11, Chapter 1 (1st century AD)

“This was known to Cyrus by his reading the book which Isaiah left behind him of his prophecies… Accordingly, when Cyrus read this, and admired the Divine power, an earnest desire and ambition seized upon him to fulfill what was so written.”

Same king. Same return-and-rebuild policy. Ezra says the Lord stirred him; the Cylinder credits Marduk. These are already in the class slides — project them and let the class notice.
