Teachers —

Starting this week, I want to send you a few resources each Sunday as you prepare to teach. Please know I’m not telling you what to teach — not in any way. That belongs to you and the Lord, for the class in front of you.

This lesson can go so many beautiful directions. I’ve sat with the materials and gathered three themes I personally would lean into — one of them, or all three. Take what helps. Leave what doesn’t. I’m simply here to put a few good tools in your hands as you do this remarkable work.

Also prepared for you

Built for the room, not for teacher prep. Sparse scripture slides, place-yourself prompts, and primary sources (Cyrus Cylinder, Josephus, Sanballat’s letter) on the screen. Use what helps; skip what doesn’t.

1
 Theme

The Lord inspires people to accomplish His purposes

Go deeper — primary source

Most classes never leave Ezra’s page. Put the Cyrus Cylinder (British Museum) and Josephus on the screen — outside documents that drop Cyrus into the real ancient world. Same move as reading a court deposition in a Doctrine and Covenants lesson: let the source do the teaching.

“the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia…”

2
 Theme

“I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down”

Go deeper — primary source

Don’t paraphrase the opposition — read Sanballat’s open letter (Nehemiah 6:6–7) out loud like a document dropped on the table. Then let Nehemiah 6:3 answer it.

“I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?”

3
 Theme

When God gives a work, He strengthens our hands

Go deeper — primary source

Stay inside the text as memoir. Nehemiah 6:9 and Ezra 7:27–28 are first-person records — fear named, prayer written, strength attributed. Read them that way with the class: not as summary, but as someone leaving a witness.

“For they all made us afraid… Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands.”
