“Miserable comforters are ye all”

Job 2:11–13; 16:2; 19:25–26; 38–40; 42:5–7 · Week of August 10–16

Job’s friends show up when his life collapses. For seven days they do everything right. Then they open their mouths. Job’s review, chapter 16:

“I have heard many such things: miserable comforters are ye all.”

What the friends did

  • Explained his suffering instead of sharing it
  • Blamed him: “God must be punishing you” (Job 22:5)
  • Had a take for everything, for thirty-five chapters
  • Defended God by accusing a wounded man

What actually helped

  • Showed up in person (Job 2:11)
  • Sat in the ashes with him, seven days
  • Said nothing: “for they saw that his grief was very great” (2:13)
  • God’s verdict: the silent friends spoke wrong; raw, honest Job spoke right (42:7)

Meanwhile, Job’s testimony

“For I know that my redeemer liveth… yet in my flesh shall I see God.” (Job 19:25–26. Said from the ash heap, before anything got better.)

“The Son of Man hath descended below them all. Art thou greater than he?” (D&C 122:8)

Talk about it

  1. Your friend’s week just fell apart. What do you do in the first ten minutes?
  2. What would you still believe on your worst day?
  3. When did someone’s presence mean more than their advice?
