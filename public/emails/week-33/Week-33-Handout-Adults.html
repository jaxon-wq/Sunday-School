Yet will I trust in him

Job 1–3; 12–14; 19; 21–24; 38–40; 42 · Week of August 10–16

A study page for the week: three anchors and one pairing worth your quiet time.

Anchor 1 · Job 19:25–26

“For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: and though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God.”

Redeemer is goel: the nearest kinsman who buys back what a family has lost (Leviticus 25:25; Boaz in Ruth 4). Job says it from the ash heap, before anything is restored. He is claiming a kinsman who will stand up for him at the last day, even if every other court fails. Handel opened the resurrection section of Messiah with these words; they have been sung at Easter for nearly three centuries.

Anchor 2 · Job 2:13 and 42:7

“They sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him… ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath.”

The friends were perfect for seven days, then explained for thirty-five chapters. In the epilogue God rebukes the orthodox explainers and vindicates the honest sufferer. Honesty with God is safer than polish about God. For those of us who minister: comfort first; explanations later, or never.

Anchor 3 · Job 38:4, 7; 42:5

“Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? … I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee.”

God never explains, and never mentions the scene of chapters 1–2. Job dies in the story not knowing what we the readers know. What he gets is God Himself, and it is enough.

Read side by side · Liberty Jail, March 1839

“O God, where art thou?” (D&C 121:1) is Job’s question, asked again by Joseph Smith while the Saints were driven from Missouri. The answer: “peace be unto thy soul… all these things shall give thee experience, and shall be for thy good. The Son of Man hath descended below them all” (D&C 121:7; 122:7–8). Two men in hard places, one pattern: perspective, presence, promise. Not an explanation.

If you want one talk: Elder Dale G. Renlund, “Infuriating Unfairness” (April 2021). “Do not let unfairness harden you or corrode your faith in God… Rather than becoming bitter, let Him help you become better.”

A question for the week: what do you know that doesn’t depend on getting an explanation?
