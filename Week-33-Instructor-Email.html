“Yet Will I Trust in Him”

Job 1–3; 12–14; 19; 21–24; 38–40; 42

Teachers,

First: teacher council is Sunday, August 23. That’s a Priesthood/Relief Society week, so there are no Sunday School classes. We’ll use second hour for the council instead: teachers, substitutes, anyone thinking about teaching. If something is working in your class, bring it. Details over the next two Sundays.

Now this week. If you’re teaching, you have Job. Heavy book, and it’s the one week where half the room is quietly living the material. As always: I’m not telling you what to teach. That’s between you and the Lord. But three things kept pulling me back as I read, in case they help.

Job 19:25–26. “I know that my redeemer liveth.” He says it from the ash heap, before anything gets better. In the law of Moses a redeemer was the nearest kinsman who bought back what a family had lost (Leviticus 25:25), the same word behind Boaz in Ruth 4. Job is saying: family is coming for me, even if every other court fails. Read it

Job 2:13. The friends sit with him in silence for seven days, and it’s the best thing they ever do. Once they start explaining, it goes downhill. By the end, God tells the explainers they got it wrong and Job got it right (42:7). Comfort first. Explanations later, or never. Read it

Job 38–42. When God finally answers, He doesn’t explain. He walks Job through the creation: the sea, the stars, the wild things. Job never learns why. “Now mine eye seeth thee” (42:5). Joseph Smith got the same kind of answer in Liberty Jail (D&C 121–122), worth reading side by side. Read it · D&C 122:7–8

Teaching the younger classes? I’d camp on 2:13 and 42:7. The whirlwind works for everyone.

William Blake, When the Morning Stars Sang Together, 1825. Job is the small one at the bottom. Worth showing your class.

If you want one talk, Elder Renlund’s “Infuriating Unfairness” (April 2021) is this whole book in fifteen minutes. And if you sing anything, hymn 136 takes its first line straight from Job 19:25.

No homework. Use what helps, skip the rest. Thanks for teaching, and thanks for handling this one with care. Somebody in your room needs it.

Jaxon
